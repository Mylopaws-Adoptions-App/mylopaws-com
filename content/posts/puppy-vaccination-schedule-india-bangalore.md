If you've just got a puppy in Bangalore, the vaccination schedule is one of the most important things to get right — and one of the most confusing. Different vets give slightly different timelines, and some vaccines that matter in India don't show up on Western charts at all.

Here's the complete, India-specific puppy vaccination schedule: what to vaccinate against, when, and why each one matters.

> **Your vet decides your puppy's exact schedule.** Brands, your puppy's age at adoption, the mother's vaccination history and local disease risk all change the plan. Use this guide to understand the schedule and ask better questions — not to replace a vet visit.

## Why vaccination is non-negotiable in India

India has large community-dog populations, mixed urban and rural environments, and tropical disease carriers like ticks and rodents. The diseases puppy vaccines protect against — **parvovirus, distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis and rabies** — aren't theoretical risks here. They circulate actively in Bangalore, and a single missed vaccination can be fatal.

Puppies are most at risk **between 6 and 16 weeks of age.** The vaccination schedule is designed to bridge exactly that window.

## The puppy vaccination chart for India

| Puppy's age | Vaccine usually given | Protects against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **6–8 weeks** | First combination vaccine (DHPPi, "4-in-1") | Distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, parainfluenza |
| **10–12 weeks** | Second DHPPi + first leptospirosis vaccine | The above + leptospirosis |
| **12–16 weeks** | Final DHPPi + lepto booster + first anti-rabies vaccine | All of the above + rabies |
| **6 months – 1 year** | Booster of the combination vaccine, as your vet advises | Locks in long-term protection |
| **Every year after** | DHPPi, leptospirosis and rabies boosters | Lifelong protection |

**The rule vets follow worldwide:** start at 6–8 weeks, repeat every 2–4 weeks, and give the last puppy dose at 16 weeks or older. Before that age, antibodies from the mother can block the vaccine from working — which is why one early shot is never enough.

### 6–8 weeks: first vaccination (DHPPi)

The first major vaccine, given as the mother's antibodies begin to fade. **DHPPi** stands for:

- **D — Distemper:** a fatal viral disease with no cure
- **H — Hepatitis** (canine adenovirus)
- **P — Parvovirus:** highly contagious and often fatal in puppies
- **Pi — Parainfluenza:** one of the causes of kennel cough

### 10–12 weeks: second vaccination (DHPPi + leptospirosis)

A DHPPi booster plus the first **leptospirosis** vaccine. Lepto matters especially in India: the bacteria live in water and soil contaminated by rodent urine, and they're common in Bangalore, particularly during the monsoon.

### 12–16 weeks: third vaccination + rabies

The final DHPPi booster, a lepto booster and the **first rabies vaccine**. Rabies vaccination is essential in India, and a vaccinated puppy needs around a month to build full rabies immunity.

### Annual boosters (from year two)

- **DHPPi booster** — yearly, or as your vet's protocol advises
- **Leptospirosis booster** — yearly (some vets advise twice a year in high-risk areas)
- **Rabies booster** — yearly

## What each disease does

- **Parvovirus** — the disease Bangalore vets see most in unvaccinated puppies. Severe vomiting and bloody diarrhoea dehydrate a puppy within hours. The virus survives for months on pavements and shoes, so a puppy can catch it without ever meeting a sick dog.
- **Distemper** — attacks the lungs, gut and nervous system. Early signs look like a cold; it can progress to seizures.
- **Hepatitis** — a viral infection of the liver, kidneys and eyes.
- **Parainfluenza** — spreads wherever dogs gather and contributes to kennel cough.
- **Leptospirosis** — damages kidneys and liver, and **can spread to people**, so the booster protects your family too.
- **Rabies** — almost always fatal once symptoms appear, in dogs and in people.

## Optional but recommended vaccines

### Coronavirus vaccine

Causes severe diarrhoea in puppies. Often recommended in dense urban areas like Bangalore, where puppy-to-puppy contact is high. Some combination brands include it.

### Kennel cough (Bordetella)

Recommended if your dog will be boarded, go to daycare, visit dog parks or grooming centres.

### Tick fever vaccine

Available in India but with mixed effectiveness. For most Bangalore dogs, **monthly tick prevention** (spot-on, oral or collar, as your vet advises) is more reliable than the vaccine. Read our guide to [tick season in Bangalore](/tick-season-bangalore-protect-your-dog/).

## Deworming schedule for puppies

| Age | Deworming |
| --- | --- |
| 2 weeks to 3 months | Every 2 weeks |
| 3 to 6 months | Every month |
| 6 months onwards | Every 3 months |

**Don't deworm on the same day as a vaccination.** Most vets deworm a few days before, because a heavy worm load can weaken the immune response. Your vet will choose the product and dose by weight.

## What to expect at the vaccination visit

### Before the appointment

- Your puppy should be healthy — not vomiting, not lethargic, no fever. Vets postpone vaccines for unwell puppies.
- No deworming on the same day.
- Carry the vaccination booklet from any earlier shots, and a photo of it on your phone.
- Don't feed a heavy meal right before the visit.

### After the vaccination

Most puppies are fine. Mild reactions are normal: sleepiness for 12–24 hours, slight swelling at the injection site, or a mild fever.

**Call your vet immediately** for severe lethargy beyond 24 hours, vomiting or severe diarrhoea, facial swelling, hives, or difficulty breathing.

## Common mistakes Bangalore pet parents make

### 1. Taking the puppy outside too early

Until 7–10 days after the final DHPPi shot, your puppy isn't fully protected against parvo and distemper. Bangalore footpaths, parks and any area community dogs use are off-limits. This is the single most common — and most dangerous — mistake.

You can still socialise safely: carry your puppy to see traffic and people, and invite healthy, vaccinated dogs over. See our guide to [socialising your puppy in Bangalore](/socialize-puppy-bangalore-parks-playdates/).

### 2. Skipping the second or third booster

The first shot does **not** give full immunity. Skipping any shot in the series leaves your puppy vulnerable.

### 3. Changing vets or brands without sharing records

If you switch vets mid-schedule, share the previous vaccination record. Brands differ slightly in formulation and timing.

### 4. Not vaccinating adopted adult dogs

If you've adopted an adult dog with an unknown history, restart the vaccination cycle with your vet. Don't assume earlier shots count.

## What it costs in Bangalore

Costs vary by vet and vaccine brand. As a rough guide, the full three-shot puppy series plus rabies typically costs **₹3,000–₹6,000 in total**, and annual boosters **₹800–₹1,500 per visit**. It's one of the lowest-cost, highest-impact investments you'll make in your dog's lifetime health.

## Pet registration in Bangalore

Bengaluru's civic authority requires pet dogs to be registered, with a rabies vaccination certificate from a vet. In 2026 the Greater Bengaluru Authority proposed annual licences for dogs over three months old, tied to rabies vaccination; the rules were in draft at the time of writing. Check current requirements with your local corporation office or vet, and keep your dog's card up to date either way.

## Getting to the vet safely

An unvaccinated puppy shouldn't walk through clinic waiting rooms, pavements or auto stands. Carry your puppy or use a carrier, avoid sitting next to visibly sick animals, and keep the journey short and clean.
