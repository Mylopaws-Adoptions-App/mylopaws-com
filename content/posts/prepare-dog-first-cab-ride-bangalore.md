For many dogs, the first car ride is to the vet. No wonder so many learn to hate cars. With a little preparation, the first ride can be the start of a lifetime of easy trips.

## Start before you book

**A few days ahead,** let your dog explore a parked car with the doors open. Toss treats inside. Let them climb in and out on their own terms. Stop while they are still enjoying it.

**Next,** sit in the car with them with the engine running for a few minutes. Treats, calm voice, no pressure.

**Then** try a short ride around the block, ending somewhere fun: a park, not the vet.

## On the day of the ride

1. **Skip the big meal.** Feed lightly three to four hours before, to reduce car sickness.
2. **Walk first.** A good walk burns nervous energy and empties the bladder.
3. **Use a harness, not just a collar.** A harness is safer if the cab brakes suddenly.
4. **Bring a familiar blanket** that smells of home for them to lie on.
5. **Carry water** and a small towel in case of drool or sickness.

## In the cab

- **Sit with your dog** in the back. Keep a calm hand on them.
- **Keep windows partly open** for air, but never wide enough for a head to go out.
- **Speak normally.** If you are anxious, your dog will be too.
- **Reward calm behaviour** with small treats.

## Signs your dog is not coping

Excessive drooling, yawning, lip licking, shaking, whining or trying to climb into the front are signs of stress or nausea. Ask the driver to pull over safely, let your dog out on a leash for a few minutes, and shorten the trip if needed. Talk to your vet if car sickness continues.
