Few breeds are as instantly recognisable as the German Shepherd. Loyal, intelligent and endlessly trainable, they're police dogs, army dogs, guide dogs — and, alongside the Labrador and Golden Retriever, one of the breeds that has long dominated Indian homes.

They're also a big commitment. A German Shepherd needs more exercise, training and grooming than most first-time pet parents expect, and the breed has health risks worth knowing before you choose a puppy. This guide covers all of it, with Bangalore-specific advice.

## German Shepherd at a glance

| Trait | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Origin** | Germany, developed in the 1890s as a herding dog |
| **Height** | Males 60–65 cm, females 55–60 cm at the shoulder |
| **Weight** | Males 30–40 kg, females 22–32 kg |
| **Lifespan** | 9–13 years (median about 10 in a large UK study) |
| **Fur** | Double layer; short-haired and long-haired types |
| **Colours** | Black and tan, sable, black, black and red |
| **Energy** | High — 1.5 to 2 hours of activity a day |
| **Good for first-time parents?** | Only if you're ready to train consistently |

## Temperament: what living with one is really like

German Shepherds are **people dogs**. They bond closely with their family, follow you from room to room, and don't do well left alone for long hours.

- **Highly intelligent.** They're consistently ranked among the top three most trainable breeds. They learn fast — including bad habits.
- **Naturally protective.** They're alert and bark at strangers and unusual sounds. Early socialisation keeps this protective instinct from turning into fear or aggression.
- **Need a job.** Without daily physical and mental exercise, they invent their own: chewing furniture, digging, barking at every passer-by.
- **Gentle with their own family,** including children — when well socialised and supervised.

**Behaviour matters for health, too.** In a large UK study of German Shepherds under veterinary care, aggression was among the five most common problems recorded. Good socialisation and reward-based training are the best prevention.

## Choosing a puppy (or adopting)

A healthy, well-tempered German Shepherd starts with healthy, well-tempered parents.

- **Meet the mother** and, if possible, the father. Calm, confident parents usually mean a calmer puppy.
- **Ask for hip and elbow screening results** for both parents. Hip and elbow dysplasia are inherited.
- **Ask for Kennel Club of India papers** and the puppies' vaccination and deworming records.
- **See where the puppies are raised.** Clean, social, in a home — not a cage in a shop.
- **Walk away** from sellers who won't let you visit, have many breeds for sale, or push you to decide fast.
- **Pick a puppy at 8 weeks or older.** Earlier separation from the litter causes behaviour problems.
- **Consider adoption.** Bangalore rescues regularly rehome German Shepherds, including adults whose temperament you can see first.

## Exercise and training

Plan for **1.5 to 2 hours of activity every day**, split into:

- Two brisk walks — early morning and evening in Bangalore's warmer months
- Off-lead running or fetch in a safe, enclosed space
- 10–15 minutes of training or puzzle games — mental work tires them as much as physical work

**Start training on day one.** Use reward-based methods — treats, praise and play. Harsh corrections make sensitive Shepherds anxious and reactive.

**Socialisation between 8 and 16 weeks matters more than anything else you'll do.** Let your puppy safely meet people of all ages, sounds, vehicles, lifts and vaccinated, friendly dogs. Until vaccinations are complete, carry your puppy rather than letting it walk on public ground — see our [puppy vaccination schedule](/puppy-vaccination-schedule-india-bangalore/).

**Protect growing joints.** Until about 12–18 months, avoid long runs on hard surfaces, repeated stair-climbing and jumping off heights. Free play on grass is fine.

## Feeding a German Shepherd

### Puppies (2–18 months)

- Feed a **complete large-breed puppy food.** It controls calcium and calories so bones grow at a steady pace — fast growth raises the risk of hip and elbow problems.
- **3–4 meals a day** until about 6 months, then 2 meals.
- Stay on puppy food until **12–18 months**, as your vet advises.
- **Don't add calcium supplements** to a complete puppy food. Extra calcium harms large-breed puppies.

### Adults

- Two measured meals a day. Use the pack's feeding guide as a starting point, then adjust to keep your dog lean — you should be able to feel the ribs easily under a thin layer of fat.
- Look for a named animal protein as the first ingredient, and omega-3 fatty acids (EPA and DHA) for skin, fur and joints.
- **Weigh food,** don't scoop it. Weigh your dog monthly.

### Reduce the risk of bloat

German Shepherds are deep-chested, which puts them at risk of **gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat)** — a life-threatening emergency where the stomach fills with gas and can twist.

- Feed two or more smaller meals instead of one big one
- No vigorous exercise for an hour before and after meals
- Discourage gulping — use a slow-feeder bowl

**Signs of bloat:** a swollen, tight belly, retching without bringing anything up, drooling, restlessness and distress. Go to a 24/7 vet immediately — see our guide to [vet hospitals in Bangalore by area](/vet-hospitals-bangalore-by-area/).

### A sensitive stomach is common

Diarrhoea is one of the problems vets record most often in German Shepherds. Change foods gradually over 7–10 days, avoid rich table scraps, and see a vet if diarrhoea lasts more than a day or two, contains blood, or comes with weight loss.

## Grooming: managing the shedding

German Shepherds shed **all year**, and twice a year they shed their dense under-fur heavily.

- **Brush 3–4 times a week** with a de-shedding rake and a slicker brush; daily during heavy shedding.
- **Bathe every 4–6 weeks,** not more — over-bathing dries the skin. Use a dog shampoo and rinse thoroughly.
- **Never shave a German Shepherd.** The double fur protects against heat and sun, and may not grow back normally.
- **Dry thoroughly** after baths and monsoon walks. Damp under-fur against the skin is how hot spots start.
- **Check and clean ears weekly.** Ear infections are the single most common problem vets record in the breed. Redness, smell, discharge or head-shaking needs a vet visit.
- **Trim nails** every 3–4 weeks.

## The health problems vets see most

A large UK study of German Shepherds under primary veterinary care found the most commonly recorded problems were:

1. **Ear infections** (otitis externa)
2. **Osteoarthritis**
3. **Diarrhoea**
4. **Overweight and obesity**
5. **Aggression**

The most common causes of death were **musculoskeletal disorders** and **being unable to stand** — a reminder that joint and spine health shapes a German Shepherd's later years.

### Breed-related conditions to know

| Condition | What it is | What to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hip and elbow dysplasia** | Malformed joints that lead to arthritis | Stiffness after rest, bunny-hopping, reluctance to climb stairs |
| **Bloat (GDV)** | Stomach fills with gas and may twist | Swollen belly, unproductive retching — emergency |
| **Degenerative myelopathy** | Progressive spinal cord disease in older dogs | Weakening hind legs, scuffing toenails, wobbling |
| **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency** | Pancreas stops producing digestive enzymes | Weight loss despite a big appetite; large, pale, greasy stools |
| **Skin infections and allergies** | Shepherds are prone to deep skin infections and allergic skin | Itching, red skin, smell, recurring ear infections |
| **Perianal fistula** | Painful openings around the anus | Licking the rear, straining, bleeding |

**Keep your Shepherd lean.** Extra weight puts more strain on joints that may already be vulnerable, and makes arthritis worse.

**From age seven,** book vet check-ups every six months, and ask about joint supplements and pain management early if you notice stiffness.

## Bangalore-specific care

- **Heat:** Bangalore's normal April maximum is about 34°C, and recent Aprils have run hotter. Walk before 8 am and after 6 pm, and check the pavement with the back of your hand — if it's too hot for you, it's too hot for paws.
- **Ticks:** dense fur hides ticks well. Check daily after walks and keep your dog on year-round tick prevention. See our [tick season guide](/tick-season-bangalore-protect-your-dog/).
- **Monsoon:** dry the dense under-fur fully after rain. Read [monsoon pet care in Bangalore](/monsoon-pet-care-bangalore-dog-skin/).
- **Apartments:** a Shepherd can live in a flat if it gets enough exercise. Keep your dog leashed in common areas, and see our [dog breeds guide](/dog-breeds-india-best-for-bangalore/) for your rights in housing societies.
- **Licensing:** Bangalore's civic body has proposed annual licensing, microchipping and rabies certificates for pet dogs. Keep your dog's rabies vaccination up to date either way.

## Is a German Shepherd right for you?

**A great fit if you:** are home a lot, enjoy daily walks and training, want a loyal, protective companion, and don't mind fur on everything.

**Think twice if you:** work long hours away from home, want a low-maintenance dog, or can't commit to training.
