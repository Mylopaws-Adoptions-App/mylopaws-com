A road trip with your pet is wonderful when it is planned and stressful when it is not. Use this checklist in the week before you leave.

## One week before

- [ ] **Vet check** if your pet is senior, has a health condition, or has not travelled before
- [ ] **Vaccinations up to date,** especially rabies. Carry the vaccination record.
- [ ] **Tick and flea preventive** applied or given on schedule
- [ ] **Confirm your stay** allows pets, and where they can go on the property
- [ ] **Find a vet** near your destination and save the number
- [ ] **ID tag** with your phone number on your pet's collar

## Documents

- Vaccination record (a photo on your phone and a printed copy)
- Any prescriptions for medication your pet takes
- Your stay's booking confirmation mentioning your pet

## Pack for your pet

- **Food:** enough for every day, plus one extra day. Do not switch food on a trip.
- **Water:** a bottle and a travel bowl for the car
- **Bedding:** a familiar blanket or bed that smells of home
- **Leash, harness and a spare leash**
- **Carrier** for cats and small dogs
- **Poop bags, wipes and old towels**
- **Medication** and a basic first-aid kit (gauze, bandage, antiseptic, tick remover)
- **Toys and a chew** to settle them in a new place

## On the day

1. **Light meal three to four hours before leaving.** A full stomach makes car sickness more likely.
2. **A long walk** before you set off, so your pet is tired and has relieved itself.
3. **Secure your pet** in a harness, crate or carrier. Never let a pet ride in the front seat or with its head out of the window.
4. **Keep the car cool and ventilated.** Never leave a pet alone in a parked car, even for a few minutes.
5. **Stop every two to three hours** for water and a short walk, on a leash.

## At the destination

- Walk the boundary first and look for gaps in fences.
- Set up your pet's bed, water and food in one corner so they have a base.
- Keep to your normal feeding and walking times.
- Check for ticks every evening, especially after walks in grass or plantations.

## Signs to stop and get help

Heavy panting that does not settle, repeated vomiting, pale gums, collapse or a dog who will not stand up: stop, cool your pet down and contact the nearest vet.
