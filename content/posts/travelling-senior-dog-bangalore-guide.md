Senior dogs, usually from around seven or eight years old (earlier for large breeds), often still love a change of scene. They just need the trip planned around them.

## What changes with age

- **Joints:** jumping into a car, slippery floors and long periods in one position become uncomfortable.
- **Bladder:** older dogs need toilet breaks more often.
- **Temperature:** seniors handle heat and cold less well.
- **Senses:** reduced sight or hearing makes new places more confusing.
- **Health conditions:** heart, kidney or joint problems may need a vet's view before travel.

## Before you go

1. **See your vet,** especially for a long trip. Ask about pain relief for stiff joints, and whether travel is advisable.
2. **Pack medications** for every day plus extra, in their original packs.
3. **Choose a closer destination.** Two to three hours is easier than six.
4. **Pick a ground-floor room** at your stay. Stairs are hard for older dogs.

## On the road

- **Help them in and out.** Lift, or use a ramp. Do not let them jump.
- **Soft, thick bedding** on the seat or floor.
- **Stops every one and a half to two hours** for a toilet break and gentle walk.
- **Keep the car cool.** Seniors overheat faster.
- **Keep their routine:** meals and medication at the usual times.

## At the destination

- Lay out their bed, water and food in a quiet corner.
- Put a rug on slippery floors where they will walk.
- Let them rest. A senior dog's holiday is mostly naps somewhere new.

## When to stay home

If your dog is unwell, in pain that is not controlled, very anxious in new places, or your vet advises against it, a calm home-style stay may be kinder. For cats, see [Mylopaws Homes](/homes/).
