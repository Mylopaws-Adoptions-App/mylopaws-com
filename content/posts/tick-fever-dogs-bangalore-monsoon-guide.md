## Nearly 1 in 2 Indian dogs carries a tick-borne pathogen. Here's what every Bangalore pet parent needs to know.

If your dog has been more tired than usual this monsoon, off their food, or running a quiet fever, a single tick bite may be the reason. A peer-reviewed survey of 525 dogs across India found that **49.7%, almost one in two, tested positive for at least one tick-borne pathogen** (Abd Rani et al., *Parasites & Vectors*). In the same study, dogs carrying ticks were **3.3 times more likely** to be infected than dogs without them.

That is not a rural, stray-dog problem. It is a Bangalore apartment problem too, and this guide explains why, what to watch for, and how Mylopaws Dermacare treats the skin beneath the fur, not just the fur on top.

## What is tick fever, exactly?

"Tick fever" is an umbrella term for several diseases a tick passes into your dog's bloodstream when it bites. In India, the two most common are:

- **Ehrlichiosis** (*Ehrlichia canis*), a bacterial infection that attacks white blood cells. Extremely common in India. Treated with the antibiotic **doxycycline**, typically for about 28 days, per the ACVIM Consensus Statement.
- **Babesiosis** (*Babesia* species), a parasite that destroys red blood cells and causes anaemia. It needs a different, prescription antiparasitic (imidocarb), given by a vet.

The carrier in almost every Indian case is the **brown dog tick, *Rhipicephalus sanguineus***.

## Why even indoor Bangalore dogs are at risk

Here is the part most pet parents get wrong. Most tick species live outdoors. The brown dog tick does not, it **survives and breeds indoors**, in carpets, sofa seams, skirting boards and pet bedding. That means a dog who "never really goes out" is still exposed, because ticks hitchhike inside on shoes, clothes and visiting pets.

Bangalore's warm, humid climate is a year-round breeding ground, and **the monsoon and post-monsoon months (roughly July to October) see the highest tick activity of the year.** A single missed monthly spot-on treatment is enough for one tick to attach, feed, and transmit disease.

## The signs to watch for

Tick fever often looks like "just an off day" at first, which is exactly why it gets missed. See a vet early if you notice:

- Lethargy or sudden tiredness
- Loss of appetite
- Fever
- Pale gums (healthy gums are pink)
- Dark or reddish urine
- Nosebleeds or pinpoint spots on the skin
- Limping or reluctance to move

**Pale gums, dark urine or a nosebleed mean a same-day vet visit.** Babesiosis in particular can destroy red blood cells quickly.

## How to protect your dog this monsoon

1. **Year-round prevention, not just monsoon.** Because the brown dog tick breeds indoors, protection can't be seasonal. Use a vet-approved monthly spot-on or an appropriate preventative, consistently.
2. **Daily tick checks in monsoon.** Run your fingers through the coat, paying attention to ears, between the toes, the neck and under the collar.
3. **Keep bedding and home dry.** Ticks thrive in moisture. Wash and dry bedding regularly.
4. **Be careful after parks, groomers and boarding.** These are common pick-up points.
5. **Book a skin-and-fur assessment if you see scratching, hair-fall or hot-spots**, don't wait for the fever.
