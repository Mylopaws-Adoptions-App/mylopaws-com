Bangalore gets two rainy spells: the southwest monsoon from roughly June to September, and northeast rains in October and November. For dogs, damp months mean skin trouble.

## What the rains do to skin

- **Fungal infections** thrive when fur stays damp, especially in skin folds, ears and paws.
- **Hot spots,** red, moist, painful patches, can appear quickly when wet fur traps heat.
- **Ticks and fleas** multiply in thick, wet vegetation.
- **The monsoon smell:** bacteria and yeast grow in fur that never fully dries.
- **Paw problems:** cracked pads and infections between the toes from walking on wet, dirty roads.

## A simple monsoon routine

1. **Dry thoroughly after every walk.** Towel, then a cool hair dryer if your dog tolerates it. Pay attention to armpits, belly, paws and ears.
2. **Wipe paws** with a clean damp cloth and dry between the toes.
3. **Check ears weekly** for redness, smell or discharge.
4. **Keep bedding dry** and wash it more often.
5. **Do not over-bathe.** Bathing more often in the rains can make things worse unless you dry completely.
6. **Stay on tick prevention.**
7. **Brush regularly** so the coat dries faster and you spot problems early.

## Breeds that need extra care

Dogs with thick double coats (Golden Retrievers, Huskies, German Shepherds), long coats (Shih Tzus, Lhasa Apsos) and deep skin folds (Pugs, Bulldogs) hold moisture longer.

## Signs to act on

Redness, a sour or yeasty smell, hair loss in patches, constant licking of one area or a moist, red patch that spreads: these need attention before they get worse. See a vet if there is pain, swelling or broken skin.
