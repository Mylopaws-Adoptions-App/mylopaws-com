Grooming is not just a haircut. A good session looks after your dog's skin, keeps them calm and catches problems early. A bad one can leave your dog anxious, nicked or with irritated skin.

Ask these seven questions before you book.

## 1. Do you check the skin before you start?

A good groomer looks at the skin, ears and paws first, and tells you what they see. If the answer is "we just bathe and cut", keep looking.

## 2. Which products do you use, and why?

Ask whether shampoos are made for pets and matched to skin type. Human shampoos and one-product-for-all are red flags.

## 3. How do you handle nervous or difficult dogs?

Look for breaks, patience, treats and a willingness to stop. Rough restraint teaches dogs to fear grooming for life. This matters most for puppies; see [stress-free puppy grooming](/therapy-puppy-grooming/).

## 4. Will my dog be left in a cage dryer?

Unattended heated cage dryers have caused serious overheating. Ask how dogs are dried and whether they are supervised.

## 5. What happens if you find a problem?

Ticks, a lump, an ear infection: you want a groomer who tells you and suggests the right next step, including a vet if needed.

## 6. Can I see where my dog will be groomed?

Clean, calm, well-ventilated spaces are a good sign. Many dogs waiting in a noisy room is not.

## 7. Do you do more than grooming?

Persistent itching, smell, dandruff and shedding are skin problems, not grooming problems. A salon that can treat skin saves you going round in circles.
